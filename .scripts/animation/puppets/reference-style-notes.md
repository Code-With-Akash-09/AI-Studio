# Reference style notes: "Easygoing Territory Defense by the Optimistic Lord" Ep 05 (Telugu dub, Muse India)
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2hhmnzdDuUE (23:40, TV anime, licensed dub). Studied only for film grammar; no characters,
designs or content are reused. Metadata in ref.info.json. No chapters. No real subtitles (only auto-translated captions, and those were rate-limited).
Frames every 2 s in frames/, contact sheets in sheets/, cut times (ffmpeg scene > 0.30) in cuts.txt.

## Pacing / editing
- 412 shots in 1420 s: **median shot 2.5 s**, mean 3.4 s, 25% of shots under 1.3 s. A cut happens on nearly every line or reaction.
- Classic dialogue coverage: establishing wide -> two-shot / over-the-shoulder -> alternating close-ups of speaker and listener -> reaction shot.
- Reaction shots of the listener are as frequent as shots of the speaker.

## Framing
- Most shots are **medium close-ups (chest-up) or close-ups**. Faces fill about 1/3 to 1/2 of the frame height. Full-body framing is used only for
  establishing and group shots.
- Over-the-shoulder two-shots: the listener's back of head or shoulder sits big and soft in the foreground, the speaker sits at a third.
- **Insert shots of props/objects** fill the frame (a food plate, a shaved-ice machine or bowl) when the dialogue mentions them.
- Extreme close-ups of eyes for drama. Comedy beats get speed lines and coloured backgrounds.

## Acting
- Characters **talk to each other, never to camera**. Eyelines cross between the two characters.
- Expression changes on almost every line (surprise -> smile -> deadpan). Big readable eyes and brows. Small head tilts.
- Gestures are few but clear: hand to chin (thinking), hands up holding an object, pointing. Holds are short, then a new pose comes in the next shot.
- Between lines, cuts hide pose changes, so the rig never has to "travel" far inside one shot.

## Backgrounds / look
- Detailed painted backgrounds, a bright and clean palette, soft blur behind close-ups (shallow depth of field). Characters are crisp with clean edges.
- Two to many characters per shot. Close-ups usually show one face, sometimes with a second partly in frame.

## Applied to our talk tests (v3)
1. Dialogue between Kabir and Kamla Dadi (no talking to camera), with eyelines toward each other.
2. Five shots of about 2-4 s each: wide establishing -> medium two-shot -> close-up insert on the key -> Dadi close-up (OTS) -> medium two-shot.
3. Each line has its own pose and expression. Transitions inside a shot are eased; bigger changes happen across cuts.
4. The prop (magic key) is shown big in a close-up when it is mentioned.
5. Close framing (chest-up / knees-up), no big foreground clutter, background soft in close-ups.
